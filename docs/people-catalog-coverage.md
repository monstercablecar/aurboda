## Content boundaries and gaps

- Infancy: age-specific play, selected developmental observations, sleep/transport/food safety and selected urgent illness signs are covered. No feeding prescription, growth interpretation, vaccine schedule, complete emergency triage, rescue algorithm or clinical developmental scoring. Optional logs are observations only.
- CDC: exactly 12 checklist ages (2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 24, 30, 36, 48, 60 months). Selected social examples, with additional motor observations at four and six months; not full checklist reproduction. Between ages, use the previous checklist; next is a preview. The 5-year reference is retained through age 5, then ends at 6.
- WHO: 2018 nurturing-care framework, 2020 early-development recommendations, and 2019 activity/rest guidance address complementary questions. They do not establish another milestone scale. CDC monitoring and WHO support are different in scope; sleep quantity does not replace AAP sleep-environment safety.
- The supplied WHO URL ending 97892400020986 resolves as supplied. The page itself displays ISBN 978 92 4 0 002098 6; no guessed replacement URL was substituted.
- Ages 3–5: play and sleep coverage plus selected CDC monitoring. No comprehensive preschool learning curriculum.
- Ages 6–8: thinner coverage: shared interaction and movement. Specialized learning accommodations, school-transition guidance, bullying and injury-prevention detail require further verified content.
- Ages 9–11: school involvement, responsibility, reading, goals, puberty conversations, movement and peer-pressure safety. The archived CDC parenting PDF is broad education, not a standardized milestone checklist. Its historical transport wording is not imported.
- Ages 11–17: autonomy, personal space, calm communication, emotional vocabulary, praise and movement. No sexual-health, driving, substance-use or mental-health treatment protocol.
- Adults: mutual communication and connection cover adult children, parents, partners and friends. Adult romantic content begins at 18. No presumption of shared household, marriage, impairment or caregiver authority.
- Care: parent care coordination requires an explicitly selected care need and age 60+ because the verified NIA source addresses older adults. Younger parents with care needs need additional source-specific content. No medication changes, falls assessment, dementia assessment, legal directives or clinical caregiving instructions.
- Accessibility and learning needs can be stored, but condition-specific interventions are intentionally absent pending appropriate source review. No diagnosis is inferred from a selected need.
- Like Switch: five optional resource records summarize four conceptual dimensions and distance. No calibrated score, surveillance, attraction manipulation, or claim of validated predictive accuracy.
- Geography: CDC/AAP/NIH and NHTSA material has US context; WHO is international. NHS illness guidance is identified as UK clinical guidance and refers to local emergency services rather than exporting UK phone numbers. No jurisdiction-specific preventive schedules are supplied.
- Dates: last_verified is this actual development review date. Publication/review dates are stored only when directly present in inspected source material; absence is not guessed. A software release must explicitly update content versions and dates.
- Licensing: records use short original paraphrases and, for 15 verified infant sources, one attributed quotation of 5–18 words per page. Images and full copyrighted checklists are not reproduced. No blanket reuse license is claimed.
- Evidence: educational guidance, professional consensus and clinical guidance remain distinct from conceptual heuristics and author-written practical applications. A source citation does not make an adapted suggestion a clinical intervention.

## Validation

Catalog tests validate the shared schema, unique IDs, source resolution and absence of orphan sources, age bounds, official checklist ages, contradictory needs, duplicate prose, lifespan baseline coverage, and adult-only heuristic eligibility. Coverage counts are descriptive, not evidence of content quality. No database, network or AI service is required to load this catalog.

The adult resources are voluntary educational context, not an assessment of the person. NIMH's population material is not converted into an identity-based risk prediction. UN Women's 2020 pandemic-era research does not establish anyone's household responsibilities. ASHA's communication guidance is adapted for respectful everyday language and context preferences; clinical and legal-record-access material is not imported. Every targeted record explicitly prioritizes the individual's preferences over assumptions.

The school-age lower bound for nonbinary communication resources is an editorial display scope, not an asserted age at which identity develops. Below age six, the universal UNICEF guidance supports an accepting environment without imposing identity. Binary gender selections do not change child developmental, biological, safety or activity timing. The two UNICEF additions remain available to every child in their age range because their practical actions apply across genders; duplicate boy/girl versions would add no supported distinction. Play suggestions apply only when developmentally suitable.

No guidance infers sex assigned at birth, anatomy, pregnancy, menopause, orientation, diagnosis, personality, intelligence or ability. No free-text description is classified. Gender-specific clinical guidance and identity-specific advice for very young children remain deliberate gaps. Catalog tests exercise all five adult relationships, explicit context changes/clearing, unchanged general guidance, unknown/private contexts and unchanged infant milestone/safety selection.

## Infant practical guide extension (1.2.0)

Added 33 distinct records and nine new sources; six existing CDC sources were revisited. The 15 source pages have dated verification and a short official quotation stored once per source, separately from paraphrased actions. CDC milestone pages display May 15, 2026 in their headers but explicitly mark Updated/Reviewed as May 16, 2026; the explicit review date is used. NHS urgent-help guidance was reviewed August 6, 2026. Undisplayed NIH/NHTSA/NHS page dates are not invented.

New play examples cover newborn, 2–4, 4–6, 6–9, 9–12, 12–15 and 15–18 month bands. Existing milestone context, interaction cards and six safe-sleep cards are retained and categorized. Newborn comfort and ordinary separation support belong to play; medical concerns and caregiver safety are help. Developmental play and monitoring use the corrected age when recorded. Clinical help and safety use chronological age regardless of developmental correction; feeding readiness retains chronological age and explicit readiness conditions. Activities remain optional; the guide offers no intelligence gains, developmental scores or guarantee of harm prevention.

Newborn tummy play uses brief, gradually increasing awake supervised opportunities. The existing WHO floor-play card now begins at two months and retains its nonmobile-infant scope. That display boundary is editorial, not a claim that younger babies cannot benefit from floor play. Later motor opportunities follow the baby rather than force a skill. Existing developmental-concern guidance covers lost skills and asking about screening; it is not an emergency diagnosis.

Safety additions cover food preparation, meal positioning, honey, car-seat use, caregiver overwhelm, overheating, smoke exposure and consumer monitor limitations. Readiness-sensitive food advice is not an instruction to start solids at four months. Car seats do not switch direction automatically at a birthday. Selected urgent signs prompt immediate local emergency help; the guide does not replace triage or provide medication doses or rescue instructions.

Catalog tests check all infant bands through actual matching, chronological safety boundaries, newborn gradual-play wording, source provenance, unique IDs and canonical-URL copy budgets. Aggregate guidance titles/summaries/actions plus the official quote remain below 200 words for each quoted page, including reused source IDs. Counts and tables above are generated from the current catalog before optional eligibility filters; they do not certify that every developmental or safety need is covered.
